5 Signs Your HR Team Is Drowning
in Repetitive Questions

And one straightforward fix that frees them up in under 20 minutes.

A
By Alex — HR operations researcher • 8 min read

Every HR manager knows this feeling: you open your inbox on Monday morning and there are 14 emails — 11 of them are the same question you've answered 47 times this year.

"What's the PTO rollover policy?"
"Can I work from home on Fridays?"
"How do I submit an expense report?"

The employee handbook has the answers. You know the employee handbook has the answers. They could find it themselves. But they don't. And they won't. Because it's 2026 and people ask questions the way they always have — by asking.

This isn't a people problem. It's a systems problem. And it's fixable in less than an hour.

Sign #1: You Have a "Top 10 Questions" List (And It Never Changes)

If you've ever compiled a list of the most-asked employee questions, you already know what those questions are. The same 10 to 15 questions — PTO, remote work, expense reimbursement, sick days, parental leave — make up the majority of what your HR team answers every single week.

Sound familiar? You've essentially built a FAQ document. Which means you've already done the hard part of identifying what needs to be automated.

Sign #2: New Hires Generate a 3-Week Inbox Spike

Every new cohort of employees hits the same learning curve. Where's the parking policy? When do I get my laptop? What's the dress code? HR gets flooded. But here's the thing: your employee handbook answers all of these. The information exists. It's just buried in a PDF nobody reads.

Sign #3: Your HR Manager Says "I Don't Have Time" When You Ask About Strategic Work

The HR manager who wants to build a culture program, improve onboarding, or develop career paths — but can't find the time because they're firefighting FAQ emails. That's a tell. That person is not understaffed. They're overloaded with work that shouldn't require a human.

Sign #4: Answering Questions Is Your HR Team's Highest-Frequency Task

Frequency matters. If your HR team had to rank their tasks by time spent, where would "answering employee questions" fall? For most teams under 200 people, it's #1. Ahead of recruiting, performance reviews, compliance, everything. That's a workflow problem, not a priority problem.

Sign #5: You've Thought About "Someone Should Build an Internal FAQ Bot" More Than Once

If you've said this out loud, you're not alone. HR teams have been thinking this for years. The technology to actually build it was just never accessible enough. It required IT involvement, custom development, expensive vendors. Until recently.
