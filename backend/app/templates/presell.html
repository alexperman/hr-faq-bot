5 Signs Your HR Team Is Drowning in Repetitive Questions

8 min read

Every HR manager knows this feeling: you open your inbox on Monday morning and there are 14 emails — 11 of them are the same question you've answered 47 times this year.

"What's the PTO rollover policy?" "Can I work from home on Fridays?" "How do I submit an expense report?"

The employee handbook has the answers. You know it. They could find it themselves. But they don't. And they won't. Because people ask questions the way they always have — by asking.

This isn't a people problem. It's a systems problem. And it's fixable in less than an hour.

Sign #1: You Have a "Top 10 Questions" List That Never Changes

The same 10 to 15 questions — PTO, remote work, expense reimbursement, sick days, parental leave — make up the majority of what your HR team answers every single week. You've essentially built a FAQ document. Which means you've already done the hard part.

Sign #2: New Hires Generate a 3-Week Inbox Spike

Every new cohort hits the same learning curve. Where's the parking policy? When do I get my laptop? The information exists. It's just buried in a PDF nobody reads.

Sign #3: Your HR Manager Says "I Don't Have Time" for Strategic Work

The HR manager who wants to build a culture program but can't find the time because they're firefighting FAQ emails. That person is not understaffed. They're overloaded with work that shouldn't require a human.

Sign #4: Answering Questions Is Your Highest-Frequency Task

If your HR team ranked tasks by time spent, where would "answering employee questions" fall? For most teams under 200 people, it's #1. That's a workflow problem, not a priority problem.

Sign #5: You've Thought "Someone Should Build an Internal FAQ Bot"

If you've said this out loud, you're not alone. The technology to actually build it was just never accessible enough. Until recently.
